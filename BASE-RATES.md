# How to know if we're lucky or good — decided before the results

*Published August 2026, before the first resolution (~September 15). This
is the interpretive frame we commit to in advance, so that neither we nor
anyone else gets to invent one after the results arrive.*

## The ledger this covers

Nine claims are sealed for the first cycle: eight ALPHA (against stated
consensus) and one calibration (deliberately with consensus, scored
separately, never counted in the headline). Resolutions land September
2026 through January 2027. Every claim carries a pre-committed failure
condition. There is no partial credit. Misses publish same-day, same
format as hits.

## What a coin flip looks like

Our ALPHA claims are binary bets against consensus. A process with no
skill — random deviation-picking dressed in mechanism language —
confirms roughly half: **4 of 8**.

Two caveats, stated against ourselves:

- **Not all eight are equally hard.** Thresholds sit at varying
  distances from consensus. Two claims deviate by under two percentage
  points — KLA (+15% vs +13.5% expected) and Lam Research (+31% vs +29%)
  — and are easier than a coin flip. Two demand large swings — MediaTek
  (+5% vs −2.45% expected) and the SK hynix spread (≥10-point lag) — and
  are harder. "Half" is a rough anchor, not an exact null.
- **The eight are not independent.** Two ride the same memory-cluster
  thesis; several share TSMC as a driver. Correlated claims mean the
  effective sample is smaller than eight — winning and losing streaks
  are both more likely than independent arithmetic suggests.

## What we commit to reading from cycle one

| Confirmed (of 8) | Our reading, committed now |
|---|---|
| 6–8 | A strong cycle — and still not proof. Chance alone produces this **14%** of the time at n=8. We will say "encouraging," not "validated." |
| 4–5 | Indistinguishable from chance. The honest reading is *no signal yet*: the machinery worked, the edge is unproven, we continue. |
| 2–3 | A weak cycle — below chance, within luck's reach, and a prompt to examine the generation process in public at the epoch review. |
| 0–1 | Evidence of a broken process, not bad luck. We will say so in those words. |

## What would actually mean something

None of the numbers above, alone. A confirm-rate starts to carry
statistical weight around **25–30 resolved ALPHA claims** — roughly a
year of cycles. Anyone quoting a rate off the first eight — including
us — is telling a story, not reporting evidence. This note exists so
that in January we are on the record saying that *before* we knew the
answer.

## The calibration claim

TSM's September print, agreeing with consensus (≥40% growth), tests
something different: whether the machine can recognize when the street
is *right*. It feeds a separate calibration rate. A machine that only
ever disagrees is a contrarian, not an analyst.

## Unresolved claims

A claim whose data source fails or whose observable proves unmeasurable
resolves UNRESOLVED: it counts toward neither rate, but it is published
and tallied. A badly written claim is a process failure we surface, not
a mulligan.
